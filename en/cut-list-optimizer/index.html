How to use the cut list optimizer

  1. Pick the sheet. Type its length and width or tap a common size. Choose mm, cm or inches; inches can have fractions like 23 1/2.
  2. List the parts. One row per part size, with the quantity. Names are optional; empty names get the letters A, B, C.
  3. Read the layout. The plan updates as you type. Every sheet is drawn to scale with the part names and sizes on it.

Used, offcut and waste are three different things

A sheet that is 84% used is not 16% waste. Part of what is left is usually a clean offcut you can keep for the next job: a drawer bottom, a shelf, a small cabinet back. This calculator counts pieces of at least 200 × 100 mm (8 × 4 in) as offcuts and shows them in green. Waste is only what really goes in the bin: saw kerf and strips too small to use.

When two layouts need the same number of sheets, the optimizer keeps the one that leaves the bigger offcut.

Saw kerf, edge trim and grain

Saw kerf is the material the blade turns into dust in every cut, usually 3 to 4 mm (about 1/8 in). The optimizer leaves it between the parts, so the sizes you enter are the sizes you get.

Edge trim cuts a strip off every edge of the sheet first. Use it when the factory edges are damaged.

Grain: for veneered boards or melamine with a wood pattern, turn on grain and every part keeps its length along the sheet's length. Without grain, parts may be turned to fit better.

Questions

Is it free?
Yes. No sign-up, no ads, no limit on how often you use it.
Are my sizes sent anywhere?
No. The optimizer runs in your browser. The share link carries the sizes after the # sign, a part of the address that browsers do not send to servers.
Is it the same calculation as the app?
Yes. This page runs the same cutting optimizer as the 0X Cut app.
What does the web version leave out?
The page plans one sheet size with up to 300 pieces. The app adds several materials and sheet sizes in one job, offcut reuse, the cut order for your saw, PDF and labels, and it saves your jobs.
Does it work for plywood, MDF and chipboard?
Yes, and for any rectangular sheet such as glass, acrylic or aluminum composite, as long as every cut goes straight across the piece, like on a panel saw or a table saw.
I cut bars, not sheets.
Use the linear cut optimizer for bars, pipes and profiles.

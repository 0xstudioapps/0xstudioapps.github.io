How to use the linear cut optimizer

  1. Set the bar length. Type the length of the stock you buy, or tap a common one: 6 m steel and aluminum, 6.5 m PVC profile, 12 m rebar, timber lengths. Choose mm, cm or inches; inches can have fractions like 34 1/2.
  2. List the pieces. One row per piece length, with the quantity. Names are optional; empty names get the letters A, B, C.
  3. Read the plan. It updates as you type. Every bar is drawn to scale, left to right in cutting order, with the piece names and lengths on it.

Used, offcut and waste

What is left at the end of a bar is not always waste. If it is 300 mm (12 in) or longer, the plan marks it green as an offcut you can keep for the next job. Waste is what the saw turns into chips plus the short ends nobody can use. When two plans need the same number of bars, the optimizer keeps the one that leaves the longer offcuts.

Saw kerf and end trim

Saw kerf is the material the blade removes in every cut: about 3 mm for a metal chop saw or a miter saw, 1 to 2 mm for a band saw. The optimizer leaves it between the pieces, so the lengths you enter are the lengths you get.

End trim cuts a little off both ends of every bar first. Use it when the ends are bent, rusty or not square.

Questions

Is it free?
Yes. No sign-up, no ads, no limit on how often you use it.
What can I plan with it?
Anything cut to length from straight stock: steel tube and angle, aluminum and PVC window profiles, pipe, rebar, timber, curtain rails, cable trays.
Are my sizes sent anywhere?
No. The optimizer runs in your browser. The share link carries the sizes after the # sign, a part of the address that browsers do not send to servers.
Is it the same calculation as the app?
Yes. This page runs the same cutting optimizer as the 0X Cut app.
What does the web version leave out?
The page plans one bar length with up to 500 pieces. The app adds several bar lengths and your offcut stock in one job, PDF and pictures to share, and it saves your jobs.
